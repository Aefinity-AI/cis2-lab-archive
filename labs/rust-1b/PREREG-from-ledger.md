 R1 Load. "uptime load average below 0.3 and fx status clear before and after" is read as: at the start of every arm run the 1-minute load average is below 0.3 and no other leg-* or sv5-* user unit is active and no llama-bench or ggml-rpc-server process is alive (the runner waits up to 10 minutes; if the host does not settle the rep is void and repeated); at the end of the run no other such unit is active; the load average at the end is recorded in every row and is not gated, because one single-thread arm run leaves the 1-minute load average near 1.0, so a gate on it would void every rep. In place of an idle test after the run, the runner measures the other load during the run itself: it reads the busy CPU time per logical CPU from /proc/stat (user, nice, system, irq, softirq and steal, in seconds) before the arm process starts and after it ends, and subtracts the arm process's own CPU time (utime plus stime of the reaped child) and the runner's own process CPU time. The two figures of a rep are (a) that remainder on the arm's CPU or CPUs and on their hyper-threading sibling(s), together, as a fraction of the arm process's wall time, and (b) the same remainder over all logical CPUs of the host, in CPUs averaged over the arm process's wall time. The void limits are relative to an idle baseline measured in the job. After the idle gate has passed and before the opening rep (nothing timed yet, no arm running), the runner measures the baseline with the same foreign-load formula it uses for a rep: 6 windows of 20 s each. The baseline is the maximum over the 6 windows, separately for (a) and for (b). The limits are the baseline plus a margin, 2 percentage points for (a) and 0.05 CPU for (b), and never below 2 percent for (a) and 0.10 CPU for (b). A rep is void, recorded as VOID and repeated, when its figure (a) is above limit (a) or its figure (b) is above limit (b). If the baseline itself is above 15 percent for (a) or above 0.30 CPU for (b), the job ends INVALID with the reason host_not_idle and nothing is timed. The baseline (all 6 windows and the two maxima), the margins, the floors and the resulting limits are printed in the RESULT line and are carried in every row, next to the two figures of the rep; the margins, the floors and the baseline rule are fixed by this amendment, and no rep is excluded after the fact for any other load. Voids from this rule and from the idle test are counted together; more than 6 voids in one job stop the job as PARTIAL.
 R2 Clock. "CPU MHz from /proc/cpuinfo before and after each rep, a rep with more than 15 percent drop is discarded" is read as: the reading is the MHz of the CPU the arm is pinned to (R7; at N=2 the opening one of the two arm CPUs), taken from /proc/cpuinfo once a second from 2 s after the start of the arm process to its end. The reference is the median of the per-rep medians of the kept reps of that host so far (all arms and both phases); for the opening rep of a host it is that rep's own median. A rep is discarded and repeated when its lowest reading or its median is more than 15 percent below the reference, or when a thermal-throttle counter of the host changed during it (counters exist where the kernel exposes thermal_throttle in sysfs; the clock rule alone applies elsewhere). The third discard on a host makes the host INVALID, as the bar says. The reference is not the highest MHz seen anywhere in the job: on hosts with the schedutil governor and turbo (box2, box3) idle readings change by more than 15 percent within seconds, so such a reference would void the host by construction.
